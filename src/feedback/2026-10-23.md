Collected in class on 23 October 2026, in week 4 of the module. Fifty-seven of
you answered the first question, 39 the second and 17 the third.
Thank you.

### What works

Forty-seven of the 73 comments on what works name the activities: the games
keep the room awake, and several of you say that playing a game first and
meeting the theory afterwards helps with the theory. Twenty-eight
mention the website, the notes, the worked solutions or the video walkthroughs.
The enthusiasm, the questions in class and the bribentives all get a mention
too. None of that is changing.

### What does not work

One theme dominates, and it is a fair one. Thirty of the 47 comments on what has
not worked are about the mathematical content: the definitions, the theorems and the
notation in the notes, how much of them appears in class, and how clearly the
game we have just played connects to them. Several of you put it as "sometimes
it feels like we just play games", and several more said that the subgame
perfection class, where we played the Traitors and then wrote the centipede game
out on the board, was a good mix. 

Twelve comments ask for more exam-style work in class and a clearer statement of
which proofs and definitions are examinable. Six are about the games themselves,
mostly that the rules are not clear enough when you play in pairs. Five ask for
resources: the slides, a version of the notes you can download and annotate,
more past papers, and better handwriting in the videos. Three are about the
room, which I will get back to below.

### What I am doing

1. Every class will end on the notes. Each slide carries the numbered section
   of the [textbook](https://vknight.org/gtb/) it is reading, and the last slide
   will summarise them into one table, marks what is examinable, and says what to read
   before the next class.
2. More of each class will be spent turning the game into notation, the way
   the subgame perfection class did.
3. When an examinable theorem comes up, I will say so in class and point at
   the [list of examinable theorems](/assessment/).
4. The slide decks are on the site already, linked at the foot of every topic
   page, so you can read them before class and keep them afterwards. I will not
   be uploading the versions I annotate live.
5. I will add a summary of rules of each game slide to stay on screen while you play it, so you are not
   working from memory once you are in pairs.
6. The notes are linked as a PDF you can download and annotate, at the foot of
   every topic page.
7. Last year's exam paper is on the [Assessment](/assessment/) page.

### Single comments worth answering

Most of what follows was said by one person, which does not make it less worth
answering.

> If you could sometimes chuck up a definition or a theorem on the slides.

Every slide now names the numbered section of the notes it is reading, and the last
slide of each class gathers them into one table.

> It is unclear what I am meant to be taking away from each lecture. Perhaps a speedy
> summary would help?

That same closing slide is the summary, and it ends with what to read before the next
class.

> You have mentioned some topics on the website are not in the exam, can you mention
> which ones are not?

The [schedule](/) is the examinable list: if a topic is not on it, it is not in the
exam. The [Assessment](/assessment/) page lists the theorems whose proofs you need on
top of that.

> Is there any way of downloading the notes to a device so I can annotate?

Yes. The whole textbook is available as [one
PDF](https://vknight.org/gtb/pdf/main.pdf), linked at the foot of every topic page.
One warning: the PDF is a snapshot, so any typos and small mistakes in it stay in it.
I fix those in the web version as they are found, so check there if something looks
wrong.

> Please can you upload the PowerPoints used in class.

They are already on the site, linked from every topic page, so you can read a deck
before class as well as after.

> Any more past exams available from previous years?

Last year's paper is now on the [Assessment](/assessment/) page.

> Maybe more quiz questions, the short form ones at the end of lectures.

I will keep this in mind. I had intended for the quizzes to be simple things for
you to do if you had 5 minutes but they did work well during the traitors
session. When we have time I will do a quizz in class.

> Could you try making notes in the notes app with your pen? A scrolling board is
> easier to follow than an infinite whiteboard.

A fair point. I will run the next example class that way.

> I have not done discrete maths before and the notation at the start did not make
> much sense.

You were not the only one. Every symbol we use is defined in chapter 2 of the notes,
utility included, before anything is built on it. I hope you feel like you can ask me
if you're ever unsure.

> The handwriting in the videos is like reading ancient greek: beautiful but I do not
> know what it means.

Fair. I will try and do better next time.

> I personally dislike 2.26 because the tables are an odd shape. I think it is unclear
> how many people are meant to fit on a table which discourages people from making
> space for others.

I should admit that I like the room. It is one of the few teaching spaces where
everybody can see everybody else and where a game can spread across the floor, which is
most of why this module is taught the way it is. That does not make it a good room to
write in, and the tables really are an odd shape. Somebody also asked about the
temperature in there, which is a fair question.

> I like your approach to class q/a in the way that each response is genuinely
> appreciated even if it's wrong, it encourages engagement.

Thank you, and thank you to everybody who answers in class. A wrong answer out loud is
worth more than a right one kept quiet: it tells me exactly where to go next, and the
person who offers it has done the whole room a favour. That is not changing, so keep
putting your hand up.

Honeydew melon, Lidl bakery items, KitKats and fruit have all been proposed as
bribentives. Noted.

The balance I am not changing is the order: activity first, theory second. The
games are not a break from the content, they are the thing the content is about,
and the feedback above is what tells me the join needs to be tighter.
