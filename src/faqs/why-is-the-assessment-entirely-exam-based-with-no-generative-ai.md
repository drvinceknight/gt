title: Why is the assessment entirely exam based, with no generative AI allowed?
order: 4
---

The aim of the course is that *you* can do game theory: state and prove the
results, compute the equilibria and interpret what they mean. A closed-book
examination is the most direct and fair way to assess that.

The research is consistent about what happens when you hand your thinking over.
A [study in PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122) found that
students with access to GPT-4 improved on practice problems but did worse than
their peers once access was removed. A
[study of 666 participants](https://doi.org/10.3390/soc15010006) linked heavier
AI use to weaker critical thinking through *cognitive offloading*, and a
[brain-imaging study at MIT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08872) found that essay
writers using an LLM had the poorest recall of their own work. None of this is
new: [Kirschner, Sweller and Clark](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326985ep4102_1)
argued two decades ago that learning is a change in long-term memory, and that
teaching fails when it leaves the learner consulting a source instead of
building knowledge of their own.

You will certainly use AI tools in your working life, but they are most useful
to people who understand the material well enough to direct them and to catch
their mistakes. That understanding is what this course, and its examination,
are for.
