title: Why is the assessment entirely exam based, with no generative AI allowed?
order: 4
---

The aim of the course is that *you* can do game theory: state and prove the
results, compute the equilibria and interpret what they mean. A closed-book
examination is the most direct and fair way to assess exactly that, and it is
a deliberate choice meant to protect your learning.

Recent published research shows that passing your thinking on to an AI weakens
the very abilities a course is meant to build. A
[study in PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122) found that
students with access to GPT-4 improved on practice problems but performed
worse than their peers once access was removed: the tool did the learning
instead of them. A
[study of 666 participants](https://doi.org/10.3390/soc15010006) found that
heavier AI use was associated with weaker critical thinking, explained by
*cognitive offloading*, the habit of handing thinking to the tool rather than
doing it. And a
[brain-imaging study at MIT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08872) found that
essay writers using an LLM showed the weakest neural connectivity and the
poorest recall of their own work, an effect the authors call accumulating
'cognitive debt'. None of this is entirely new: a
[classic analysis by Kirschner, Sweller and Clark](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326985ep4102_1)
argued two decades ago that learning is a change in long-term memory, and that
teaching approaches fail when they leave the learner consulting a source
instead of building knowledge of their own. In short, thinking works like a
muscle: the skills you hand over, you lose.

An examination with no generative AI means the work you put in during the
course, attempting the example questions and reproducing the proofs yourself,
is precisely the work that is rewarded. You will certainly use AI tools in
your working life, but they are most valuable to people who understand the
material well enough to direct them and to catch their mistakes; that
understanding is what this course, and its examination, are for.
