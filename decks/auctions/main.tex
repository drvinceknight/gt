\slidehead{What are the rules, exactly?}
\notesref{16.2.1}{Auction Games: Definition: Auction Game}

\vspace{1cm}

{\large Everyone submits \answerrule[4cm]{one sealed bid}, without seeing
anyone else's, and the item goes to \answerrule[5cm]{the highest bidder}.}

\vspace{1.1cm}

{\large In a first-price auction the winner pays \answerrule[5cm]{their own bid}.
In a second-price auction they pay \answerrule[5cm]{the second highest bid}.}

\vspace{1.1cm}

{\large So with value \(v\), in round 1 your payoff is \(v -\)
\answerblank[3.6]{your own bid} if you win, and \answerblank[1.4]{\(0\)}
otherwise.}

\vspace{0.7cm}

{\large In round 2 your payoff is \(v -\)
\answerblank[4.6]{the second highest bid} if you win, and
\answerblank[1.4]{\(0\)} otherwise.}

  bid.\\[0.5em]
  \textbf{Round 2, second price:} roll again; the highest bid wins and pays
  the second highest bid.\\[0.5em]
  The winner scores their value minus what they pay; everyone else scores 0.
  Every winner gets \mbox{bribentives} based on their score.\\[0.5em]
  {\color{inkgrey} Afterwards, a few groups call out all three values and bids,
  winners and losers alike. Every group gives the price paid.}};

\slidehead{Second price: the result}
\notesref{16.2.3}{Auction Games: Bayesian Nash Equilibrium for Second Pay Auction}

\vspace*{\fill}

\begin{center}
\tikz\node[draw=inkteal, line width=1pt, rounded corners=4pt, fill=white,
  text width=19cm, inner sep=0.6cm, align=left, text=inktext, font=\Large]{%
  {\bfseries\color{inkteal} Theorem.} In a second-price auction, bidding your
  own value, \(b_i =\) \answerblank[1.6]{\(v_i\)}, is
  \answerrule[5cm]{weakly dominant}. It is a Bayesian Nash equilibrium for
  everyone to bid their value.};
\end{center}

\vspace{1cm}

{\Large To bid well you need to know \answerrule[5cm]{nothing at all} about the
other bidders.}

\newpage
\slidehead{First price: the result}
\notesref{16.2.4}{Auction Games: Bayesian NE in a first-price auction}

\vspace*{\fill}

\begin{center}
\tikz\node[draw=inkred, line width=1pt, rounded corners=4pt, fill=white,
  text width=19cm, inner sep=0.6cm, align=left, text=inktext, font=\Large]{%
  {\bfseries\color{inkred} Theorem.} In a first-price auction with \(N\)
  bidders whose values are spread evenly, the Bayesian Nash equilibrium is for
  each bidder to shade their bid to \(b_i =\)
  \answerblank[2.6]{\(\frac{N - 1}{N}\,v_i\)}.};
\end{center}

\vspace{1cm}

{\Large For us, \(N = 3\), so \(b = \) \answerblank[2]{\(\frac{2}{3}\,v\)}: the line
on the round 1 grid.}

\vspace*{\fill}

\newpage
\slidehead{What the seller made}
\notesref{16.2.5 and Appendix}{Revenue Equivalence; Order Statistics}

\vspace{0.6cm}

{\large The room: round 1 averaged \blank[2] and round 2 averaged \blank[2].}

\vspace{0.6cm}

{\large With three values spread evenly from 0 to 12, the highest is
\answerblank[1.4]{\(9\)} on average and the second highest
\answerblank[1.4]{\(6\)}.}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[xscale=1.3]
  \draw[inkgrey, line width=1pt] (0,0) -- (12,0);
  \foreach \x in {0,12}{%
    \draw[inkgrey, line width=1pt] (\x,-0.2) -- (\x,0.2);
    \node[below, text=inkgrey] at (\x,-0.2) {\x};}
  \onlyanswer{%
    \foreach \x/\lab in {3/lowest, 6/second highest, 9/highest}{%
      \fill[inkanswer] (\x,0) circle (0.12);
      \node[above, text=inkanswer, font=\small] at (\x,0.2) {\lab};
      \node[below, text=inkanswer] at (\x,-0.2) {\x};}
    \foreach \a in {0,3,6,9}{%
      \draw[inkanswer, decorate, decoration={brace, amplitude=4pt, mirror}]
        (\a+0.1,-0.75) -- (\a+2.9,-0.75)
        node[midway, below=4pt, font=\small] {3};}}
\end{tikzpicture}

{\small\color{inkgrey} On average, three values split the line into four equal
gaps: the \(k\)-th smallest of \(N\) averages \(\frac{k}{N + 1}\) of the way along
(Appendix: Order Statistics).}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.3cm}

{\large Second price: the seller gets the second highest value,
\answerblank[1.4]{\(6\)}. \hspace{0.8cm} First price: \(\tfrac{2}{3}\) of the
highest, \answerblank[1.4]{\(6\)}.}

\vspace{0.6cm}

{\large In both, the item goes to the highest value and a bidder with the
lowest possible value expects nothing. So on average they raise the same:
\answerrule[5.4cm]{revenue equivalence}.}
